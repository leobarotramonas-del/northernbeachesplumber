A hidden water leak does not always announce itself with a burst pipe. It may begin as unexplained meter movement, a damp smell, lower pressure or a water bill that no longer matches normal use. Early assessment can narrow the likely source before moisture causes broader damage or unnecessary surfaces are opened.

### Warning signs that deserve investigation

Some moisture comes from condensation, roofing or drainage rather than a pressurised water pipe. That is why the location, timing and pattern matter. Arrange a plumbing assessment when you notice:

- the water meter moving when all fixtures and appliances are off;
- a sudden or unexplained increase in water use;
- damp patches, mould, lifting finishes or musty odours;
- running-water sounds when nothing is operating;
- reduced pressure without an obvious fixture fault;
- warm or wet areas on a floor; or
- recurring moisture around cabinets, walls or outdoor pipe routes.

If water is actively spreading near electrical equipment, keep clear and use the property’s isolation only when it is safe to do so. For uncontrolled water, review our [urgent plumber information](/services/emergency-plumber/) and provide the full address and safety details when you call.

### A safe water-meter check

Sydney Water recommends turning off water-using appliances and fixtures, recording the meter reading, waiting at least one hour without using water and then checking the reading again. Its official [guide to spotting and fixing water leaks](https://www.sydneywater.com.au/content/dam/sydneywater/documents/spotting-and-fixing-water-leaks.pdf) explains the process and notes that a licensed plumber should handle repairs on private plumbing.

Before testing, make sure nobody will use taps, toilets, irrigation, washing machines, dishwashers or automatic refill systems. Do not shut down equipment if you are unsure how it operates. A meter that changes can support further investigation, but it does not identify the exact pipe or location by itself.

Record the following information:

- the starting and finishing meter readings;
- the test duration;
- areas where moisture or sound is strongest;
- recent building, garden or plumbing work;
- whether the symptom changes with rain; and
- any known underground, subfloor or shared services.

### What professional leak detection involves

Leak detection is a process of narrowing possibilities. A plumber may inspect fixtures and accessible connections, isolate sections, assess pressure and compare moisture evidence. The suitable method depends on the property, pipe material, access and symptoms. No single tool can guarantee a precise result in every building.

### When to call a licensed plumber

Call when a meter test suggests ongoing use, moisture is worsening, pressure has changed or the source remains unclear. Prompt advice is especially important when water is near electrical equipment, damaging finishes or affecting another lot. Avoid cutting into walls or floors without a reasoned inspection plan.
