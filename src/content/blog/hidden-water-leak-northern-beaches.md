The hardest leaks are rarely the ones spraying in plain sight. A hidden leak may show itself as a meter that moves overnight, a damp smell in one room or pressure that no longer feels normal. When we investigate, we do not begin by choosing a wall to open. We begin by testing whether the clues agree.

### The clues we take seriously

Moisture does not automatically mean a leaking water pipe. Roofing, condensation, failed waterproofing and outside drainage can create similar marks. Timing helps us separate those possibilities. A patch that grows only after rain tells a different story from a meter that moves while every fixture is off.

When customers call us, we ask about:

- unexplained movement on the water meter;
- a sudden change in water use or the bill;
- damp patches, mould, lifting finishes or musty odours;
- running-water sounds when nothing is operating;
- reduced pressure at several fixtures;
- warm or wet areas on a floor; and
- recent renovation, garden or plumbing work.

We also ask whether another apartment or neighbouring area is affected. That detail can change which service should be isolated first and who needs to provide access.

### The meter check we use as a starting point

A controlled meter check can confirm that water is moving when the property should be still. Sydney Water recommends turning off water-using fixtures and appliances, recording the reading, waiting at least one hour without using water and checking again. Its official [guide to spotting and fixing water leaks](https://www.sydneywater.com.au/content/dam/sydneywater/documents/spotting-and-fixing-water-leaks.pdf) explains the steps and the owner’s responsibility for private plumbing.

Before testing, account for toilets, irrigation, washing machines, dishwashers, ice makers and automatic refill systems. Do not switch off equipment if you do not understand how it operates. Record:

- the starting and finishing readings;
- how long the test ran;
- whether all occupants avoided water use;
- where moisture or sound seems strongest; and
- whether weather conditions changed.

A changed reading confirms possible ongoing use; it does not name the pipe or pinpoint the location. That distinction prevents a useful test from becoming an overconfident diagnosis.

### How we narrow the search without guessing

We work from broad evidence towards a smaller area. Depending on the property and symptoms, that can mean inspecting visible fixtures and connections, isolating accessible sections, assessing pressure and comparing moisture in likely locations. Pipe material, layout and access determine which checks are worthwhile.

No tool sees through every surface or guarantees an exact result in every building. We explain the limits of the evidence before recommending intrusive access. ### What we want you to know at the end

A useful leak assessment should leave you with three things: the evidence found, the limits of what has been confirmed and the next justified action. That may be a targeted repair, another test or access to a specific area. It should not be a vague instruction to start opening finishes everywhere.
